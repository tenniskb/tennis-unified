Footwork at Moment of Impact¶

How your feet are positioned at the exact moment of contact determines power, balance, and shot quality.

Universal Principles¶

Principle Application
Stable base Feet planted, balanced
Weight forward Transferring into shot
Hips open Rotated toward target
Front foot firm Anchor for rotation

Forehand Impact Footwork¶

Neutral Stance (Traditional)¶

  • Front foot steps toward target (45°)
  • Weight transfers back → front
  • Back foot pivots, heel up
  • Hips/shoulders rotate fully

Open Stance (Modern)¶

  • Outside foot (right for righty) loads
  • Weight stays on outside foot
  • Hips rotate around stable base
  • Inside foot may lift or stay light
  • Faster recovery for wide balls

Semi-Open Stance¶

  • Between neutral and open
  • Front foot angled, not fully forward
  • Best of both - power + recovery
  • Most common at pro level

Backhand Impact Footwork¶

One-Handed Backhand¶

  • Closed/neutral stance preferred
  • Front foot steps across (toward net post)
  • Weight transfers forward
  • Back foot anchors, drives rotation
  • Non-dominant arm extends back for balance

Two-Handed Backhand¶

  • Neutral to open stance
  • Similar to forehand mechanics
  • Front foot steps toward target
  • Both hands on racket = more stability
  • Easier open stance than 1HBH

Serve Impact Footwork¶

Platform Stance¶

  • Feet stationary throughout
  • Back foot pushes, front foot anchors
  • Weight transfers back → front
  • More stable, consistent

Pinpoint Stance¶

  • Back foot slides up to front foot
  • Narrower base at launch
  • More explosive vertical drive
  • Advanced - requires balance

Volley Impact Footwork¶

Forehand Volley¶

  • Step forward with opposite foot (left for righty)
  • Short, compact step
  • Weight transfers into shot
  • Racket leads, foot follows

Backhand Volley¶

  • Step forward with front foot (right for righty)
  • Cross-over step common
  • Shoulder turn more than forehand
  • Firm front leg = stable base

Overhead/Smash Impact¶

  • Get behind ball - critical
  • Sideways stance - like serve
  • Back foot loads, drives up
  • Front foot anchors
  • Jump if needed for height

Return of Serve Footwork¶

First Serve Return¶

  • Split step wide, low
  • Short compact swing
  • Block/redirect pace
  • Minimal foot movement - react

Second Serve Return¶

  • Step in aggressively
  • Neutral/closed stance
  • Drive the return
  • Move forward after contact

Summary Table¶

Stroke Stance Options Key Foot at Impact
Forehand Neutral, Open, Semi-open Front foot (neutral) / Outside foot (open)
1H Backhand Closed, Neutral Front foot (stepping across)
2H Backhand Neutral, Open Front foot / Outside foot
Serve Platform, Pinpoint Front foot (both)
FH Volley Step forward Opposite foot
BH Volley Step forward Front foot (cross)
Smash Sideways Back foot drives
Return Split step + react Varies by serve

Practice Focus¶

  1. Shadow strokes - check foot position at contact
  2. Freeze drill - hit, hold finish, check feet
  3. Video analysis - slow motion at contact
  4. Partner feeds - specific stance practice
  5. Match play - automatic under pressure
