# Elena Rybakina

## Overview
- **Full Name**: Elena Andreyevna Rybakina
- **Date of Birth**: June 17, 1999
- **Nationality**: Kazakhstani (born in Moscow, Russia)
- **Turned Pro**: 2016
- **Plays**: Right-handed (two-handed backhand)
- **Highest Ranking**: No. 3 (achieved June 2023)
- **Career Titles**: 13 WTA singles titles
- **Grand Slam Titles**: 1 (2022 Wimbledon)
- **Career Prize Money**: Over $15 million

## Playing Style & Strengths
**Powerful Serve**: Rybakina possesses one of the best serves on the WTA Tour, capable of generating tremendous pace and accuracy.

**Forehand Weapon**: Her forehand is struck with immense pace and flat trajectory, allowing her to dominate from the baseline.

**Two-Handed Backhand**: Solid and reliable, capable of handling pace and generating winners when needed.

**All-Court Game**: Comfortable playing from the baseline, approaching the net, and defending with her athletic ability.

**Mental Toughness**: Shown resilience in big matches, including coming back from deficits.

**Weaknesses**: Can sometimes be inconsistent with her timing; movement, while good, is not elite compared to the very best; return of serve can be a liability at times.

## Career Highlights
- **2022**: Won Wimbledon, defeating Ons Jabeur in the final, becoming the first Kazakhstani to win a Grand Slam singles title.
- **2023**: Reached Australian Open semifinal, losing to Aryna Sabalenka.
- **2021**: Won WTA 1000 title in Indian Wells.
- **Multiple WTA 500 titles**: Rome (2022), etc.
- **Olympic Participant**: Represented Kazakhstan at Tokyo 2020 Olympics.
- **WTA Finals Participant**: 2022.

## Rivalries
- **Rybakina vs. Swiatek**: Competitive matches, often with Rybakina having the edge on faster surfaces.
- **Rybakina vs. Jabeur**: Their 2022 Wimbledon final was a classic, with Rybakina prevailing.
- **Rybakina vs. Sabalenka**: Several tight matches, often going to three sets.
- **Rybakina vs. Gauff**: Power vs. athleticism matchups.

## Legacy (Early Career)
- **Kazakhstani Tennis Star**: Kazakhstan's most successful tennis player in history.
- **Wimbledon Champion**: Joined the elite group of women's singles Grand Slam champions.
- **Power Baseline Pioneer**: Demonstrated effectiveness of a big-serving, aggressive baseline game in the modern era.
- **Next Generation Leader**: Part of the group of players shaping the future of women's tennis alongside Swiatek, Sabalenka, and Gauff.

## Fun Facts & Trivia
- Born in Moscow, Russia, but represents Kazakhstan since 2018.
- Comes from an athletic family; her father Andrei was a professional ice hockey player.
- Known for her calm, composed on-court demeanor, rarely showing emotion.
- Fluent in Russian and English, with some Kazakh.
- Known for her charitable work, particularly supporting children's sports programs in Kazakhstan.
