# Breakdown of Stroke Techniques

Stroke analysis is very important for improving your tennis skills. When analysing stroke technique, you should focus on the following elements:

---

## 1. Body Movement

You should observe how the body moves throughout the stroke, from the ready position through to the finish.

- **Rotation** - hips and shoulders turn as a unit
- **Weight transfer** - from back foot to front foot
- **Balance** - maintained throughout the motion
- **Posture** - athletic, slightly forward lean

---

## 2. Arm and Racket Movement

You should observe how the arm and racket move throughout the stroke, from the ready position through to the finish.

- **Backswing** - racket taken back appropriately
- **Swing path** - low to high (topspin), high to low (slice), level (flat)
- **Contact zone** - in front of body, optimal height
- **Racket face control** - consistent through impact
- **Follow-through** - direction matches intended ball flight

---

## 3. Leg Movement

You should observe how the legs move throughout the stroke, from the ready position through to the finish.

- **Split step** - timed with opponent's contact
- **First move** - explosive first step to the ball
- **Adjustment steps** - small steps to fine-tune position
- **Loading** - weight on back leg before forward swing
- **Drive** - push off back leg into shot
- **Recovery** - quick return to ready position

---

## 4. Coordination of Body Parts

You should observe how the parts of the body coordinate with each other throughout the stroke.

- **Kinetic chain** - ground → legs → hips → torso → shoulder → arm → racket
- **Timing** - each segment fires in sequence
- **Efficiency** - no wasted motion
- **Rhythm** - smooth, continuous flow

---

## The Kinetic Chain Principle

The power in tennis strokes comes from the ground up. The sequence is:

1. **Feet/Legs** - push against the ground (ground reaction force)
2. **Hips** - rotate, transferring energy upward
3. **Torso** - rotates, adding to hip rotation
4. **Shoulder** - drives the arm forward
5. **Arm/Elbow** - extends, positioning racket
6. **Wrist/Hand** - fine control of racket face
7. **Racket Head** - final acceleration through contact

Each segment accelerates, then decelerates to transfer energy to the next segment. This is called **proximal-to-distal sequencing**.

---

## Analysis Checklist

When analyzing any stroke, check:

| Component | Key Questions |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Feet** | Split step? First move? Balance? Recovery? |
| **Legs** | Loading? Drive? Weight transfer? |
| **Hips** | Rotation timing? Range? |
| **Torso** | Rotation? Separation from hips? |
| **Shoulder** | Turn? Drive? |
| **Arm** | Extension? Path? Contact point? |
| **Wrist** | Stability? Release? |
| **Racket** | Face angle? Path? Speed? |
