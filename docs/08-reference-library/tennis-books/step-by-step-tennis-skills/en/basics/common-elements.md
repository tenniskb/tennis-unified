# Common Elements for All Strokes

There are certain elements which are common to all strokes in tennis. Understanding these fundamentals is essential before moving on to specific stroke techniques.

---

## 1. Ready Position

You should stand in a "ready" position with knees slightly bent and the racket held out in front of your body. This allows you to react quickly to your opponent's shots.

- Feet shoulder-width apart
- Weight on the balls of your feet
- Knees flexed
- Racket out front, head up
- Eyes on the ball

---

## 2. Swing Motion

You should use a smooth, efficient swing motion. This helps you generate power and control.

- Backswing: controlled, not rushed
- Forward swing: accelerate through contact
- Contact point: in front of the body
- Follow-through: complete the motion naturally

---

## 3. Contact with the Ball

You should hit the ball at the optimal height with the racket at the correct angle. This helps you generate power and control.

- **Optimal height**: usually at waist/hip level for groundstrokes
- **Racket angle**: slightly closed for topspin, open for slice
- **Sweet spot**: center of string bed
- **Timing**: ball at peak of bounce or slightly descending

---

## 4. Follow-through

You should complete the stroke smoothly and prepare for the next shot. This helps you maintain balance and readiness.

- Continue the swing naturally
- Racket finishes across the body (forehand) or over shoulder (backhand)
- Weight transfers forward
- Eyes track the ball until contact
- Return to ready position quickly

---

## Key Principles

| Element | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| **Ready position** | Quick reaction, balanced start |
| **Smooth swing** | Power + control |
| **Correct contact** | Accuracy, spin, depth |
| **Full follow-through** | Consistency, injury prevention, recovery |

---

## Common Errors to Avoid

1. **Late preparation** - racket back too late
2. **Wrong contact point** - too close, too far, too high, too low
3. **Incomplete follow-through** - stops swing early
4. **Poor balance** - weight not transferred
5. **Tension** - gripping too tight, arm stiff
