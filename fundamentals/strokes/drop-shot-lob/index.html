Drop Shot & Lob Fundamentals¶

Power wins points. Touch wins matches. The player who can change pace, spin, and height controls the opponent.

All three shots share one DNA: they use the opponent's pace against them. They're Hua Jin shots — absorb and redirect.

The Touch Q-V-ROOT-8-45-Impulse Map¶

Variable Name Touch Implementation
Q Quiet Eye Eyes park early: slice=contact zone, drop=contact zone, lob=toss or contact
V Vestibular Head still — critical for slice (side-on) and drop shot (disguise)
ROOT Rooting Slice: double Thực load; Drop: front foot holds Thực; Lob: back foot drives Thực
8 Figure-8 All tiny 8s: slice=horizontal, drop=tiny vertical, lob=tall vertical
45° Plane Slice: 45° high-to-low; Drop: 45° low-to-high then down; Lob: 45° upward
Brake Impulse No hard brake — soft absorption; Hua Jin transforms pace
Pulse Fa Jin Tiny: slice=firm hold, drop=feather touch, lob=upward brush
Jin Kình Hua dominates; Ting feels incoming spin; Fa = minimal release

Rule: Touch shots run on constant firmness (4-5/10). No gradient. Face locks early. Hua Jin — absorb and redirect.

Drop Shot¶

The drop shot isn't a soft shot — it's a disguised shot. The opponent has to think drive until it's too late.

Element Detail
Grip Continental on both sides
Face Open 20-30° at contact
Path Short, compact — high-to-low-to-high, tiny
Disguise Same prep as drive: unit turn, racket back, drop wrist at last moment
Contact In front, waist-high; absorb the pace
Target Just over net, 1-2m inside service line
Follow-through Short, freeze; racket face stays visible
When Opponent deep expecting drive — not when already in

Principle: Drop shot = 3-1 drill. Hit 3 drives, 1 drop. Same prep. Opponent cannot read it.

Coach's Tip: If you feel wrist flick, you're muscling it. Drop shot = relax — let racket head drop from gravity. Face opens. Ball kisses strings.

Fault Signals¶

Symptom Root Cause Fix
Opponent reads it Different prep from drive 3-1 drill: same prep, drop at last moment
Into net Too much cut, or face closed Open face more, softer touch, higher contact
Too long Too much pace, or face open Absorb more pace, softer hands

Lob¶

Type When Technique Target
Defensive lob Pulled wide, on the run Open face, high finish, just get it up Deep, high, buy time
Offensive lob Opponent at net, you balanced Topspin brush up, 45° plane, firm wrist Over net player, near baseline
Topspin lob Neutral or attacking Brush up back of ball, closed face Deep with kick, pushes opponent back

The offensive lob is a topspin shot, not a moonball. Brush up at 45°. Face closes through contact.

Fault Signals¶

Symptom Root Cause Fix
Short or smashed No topspin, or face open Brush up, close face, 45° plane
Too long Too much power Touch, not power — brush up

Drills¶

  1. Slice wall: 50 slices — focus on edge leading, constant face, rhythm
  2. 3-1 drill: Partner feeds; 3 drives, 1 drop; same prep; 20 cycles
  3. Drop and pass: You drop, partner runs in, you pass — trains disguise + follow-up
  4. Lob practice: Partner at net; you hit offensive topspin lobs; target = baseline corner
  5. Touch circuit: backhand slice, forehand slice, backhand drop, forehand drop, offensive lob — 5 reps each; focus on constant firmness and face control

EDGE LEADS. FACE LOCKED. ABSORB. REDIRECT. DISGUISE.
