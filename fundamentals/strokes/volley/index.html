Volley & Overhead Fundamentals¶

The compact game — no time for loops. Borrow the pace. Block. Redirect. Put it away.

The Volley Is Not a Swing¶

A volley is a block. You don't hit the ball — you meet it with a firm face and let the ball rebound. No takeback at all; racket is already set; opponent's pace supplies the energy.

The Volley Q-V-ROOT-8-45-Impulse Map¶

Variable Name Volley Implementation
Q Quiet Eye Eyes park at contact zone, 15-25cm in front; lock at opponent's contact
V Vestibular Head dead still, chin tucked; no peeking
ROOT Rooting Wide, low base; front foot steps across (not forward); tripod screws in
8 Figure-8 Tiny 8; no loop — just set, block, pulse
45° Plane Slightly open face; push through on 45° glass plane
Brake Impulse Front foot brakes hard; torso stops; racket face holds
Pulse Fa Jin 1-inch pulse; not a swing — a push through ball
Jin Kình Hua absorbs pace; Fa redirects; no Thính needed (no stretch to feel)

The Volley Checklist¶

If you swing at a volley, you're late. The racket has to be waiting. - Grip: Continental; firm wrist, constant 4/10; face sets early - Ready position: racket up, at head height, in front; split step lands wide and low - Contact: out in front, 15-25cm ahead of front foot; never at body - Face: slightly open for low volleys, neutral for high; never closed - Motion: no swing; block, absorb, 1-inch push; feel ball compress on strings - Footwork: step across on diagonal, not forward; wide base; back foot pushes - Eyes: park at contact zone at opponent's contact; hold through hit - Finish: racket face still visible; you should see your own strings afterward

Volley Types¶

Type Contact Height Face Action Target
Low volley Below net Open 15-30° Lift and push through Deep, at feet
Mid volley Waist to chest Neutral to slightly open Block, 1-inch pulse Deep, angled
High volley Above chest Slightly closed Punch down at 45° Sharp angle or put-away
Drive volley Chest-high, mid-court Closed, topspin Compact swing with topspin Winner or approach
Half-volley Off the bounce Neutral, firm Catch and lift, no swing Deep, neutralizing

Coach's Tip: "Statue volley" drill — partner feeds, you hit volley, freeze at contact for 2 seconds. If you can't freeze, you swung. 50 balls — no swing = perfect.

Overhead: The Serve You Hit in Play¶

Same kinetic chain as serve — track, move, brake, snap.

The Overhead Q-V-ROOT-8-45-Impulse Map¶

Variable Name Overhead Implementation
Q Quiet Eye Eyes track ball to contact zone; park at own contact point
V Vestibular Head still during swing; turn head with torso, never independently
ROOT Rooting Side-on; back foot loads, front foot braces; wide base
8 Figure-8 Vertical 8: racket drops behind back, crosses over, exits up and forward
45° Plane 45° diagonal; racket drops low behind, drives up through ball
Brake Impulse Front leg/hip brakes hard; torso stops; arm whips, pronates
Pulse Fa Jin 1-inch pulse at contact — pronation snap, never long swing
Jin Kình Fa = explosive snap; Hua adjusts for bad lob

Overhead Key Points¶

  1. Turn sideways immediately — non-dominant hand points at ball
  2. Move with crossover steps, never backpedaling — backpedaling = fall backward
  3. Contact: slightly in front, to right (right-hander), above head
  4. Let racket drop behind back from gravity + trunk rotation; don't muscle it down
  5. Front hip/leg brakes, torso stops, arm whips, pronates
  6. Eyes stay on contact spot until ball leaves strings; no peeking at target
  7. Finish: racket crosses body, weight forward, balanced

Coach's Tip: Overhead fear = looking at target too soon. Drill: hit overheads, call "contact" instant you feel ball on strings. Only then look. 20 balls — peek early = partner calls it.

Fault Signals¶

Shot Symptom Root Cause Fix
Volley Pops up Face too open, or swung upward Neutral face, push through not up
Volley Into net Face closed, or contact late Contact further in front, keep face neutral
Volley No control Swung, or wrist loose Constant 4/10 firm wrist, block only
Overhead Long Contact behind head, or face open Move feet, contact in front, pronate
Overhead Net Racket dropped late, or muscled down Let gravity drop racket, let trunk rotate
Overhead Falling back Backpedaled, no root Crossover steps, wide base, front foot brakes

Pre-Net Routine¶

  1. Split step: wide, low, double Hư
  2. Racket: up, head height, Continental, firm wrist
  3. Eyes: on opponent's contact zone; park early
  4. Feet: step across on diagonal; tripod screws in
  5. Contact: out in front; block, then 1-inch pulse
  6. Recover: instant Hư, then back to center

Principle: Volley and overhead give no time for SSC load. You're borrowing opponent's pace. Firm face, instant rooting, 1-inch pulse — the compact game.

RACKET UP. EYES PARK. STEP ACROSS. BLOCK. PULSE. RECOVER.
